There's a certain kind of memory every hockey parent collects. The 6 a.m. drive with the heater blasting. The smell of the gear bag in the back seat (it never fully goes away). Lacing tiny skates in a cold locker room, then watching through the glass as your kid takes a stride, wobbles, and grins. A lot of today's parents were those kids once, and now it's their turn on the other side of the glass.

Since the NHL came to Salt Lake City, more Utah kids want to play than ever. Here's how to get yours started without buying a full set of gear on day one.

Step 1: make sure they can skate

Most youth programs expect kids to stand, glide and stop before joining a team. If your child is starting from zero, begin with a learn-to-skate class. Many rinks also run learn-to-play hockey classes for kids who can skate a little.

Step 2: try it for free

USA Hockey runs Try Hockey for Free days several times a year, and Utah rinks, including Salt Lake County's, have hosted them. Kids borrow gear, get on the ice with coaches and find out whether they love it. Also ask your rink about NHL and NHLPA Learn to Play programs, which in many NHL markets include a starter set of gear.

How the levels work

Age groupWhat it looks like
6U and 8USmall-area games, cross-ice play, lots of skating and lots of fun.
10U and 12UFull-ice games begin. This is where rec and travel paths split.
14U to 18UOlder youth hockey, including high school club teams.

Age groups go by birth year under USA Hockey rules. Every player and coach needs a USA Hockey membership each season. The Utah Amateur Hockey Association is USA Hockey's affiliate for the state.

Rec or travel?

  • Rec or house league: local games, one or two ice times a week, lower cost. The right start for most kids, and honestly where a lot of the best memories happen.
  • Travel or competitive: tryouts, more practices, out-of-town tournaments and hotel pool parties. A much bigger commitment of time and money.

What it costs

Prices vary a lot between programs, so ask each one for the full number. Plan for the program fee, the USA Hockey membership, gear and skate sharpening. Rec hockey is the most affordable path, and hand-me-down or used gear cuts the start-up cost a lot. Hockey has always run on hand-me-downs. See our beginner gear checklist.

Questions to ask a program

  • How many ice times a week, and what time of day? (Those early mornings are real.)
  • Is there loaner gear for first-year players?
  • Do kids need to pass a skating level first?
  • What's the total season cost, including tournaments and jerseys?
  • Is there a girls' team or girls' program?
