Maybe you played street hockey on rollerblades in the cul-de-sac after watching The Mighty Ducks. Maybe you had a Gretzky poster on your bedroom wall, or you spent whole afternoons on a Sega hockey game with your brother. Maybe you never played at all and just fell for the Mammoth. Whatever brought you here, it's not too late. Plenty of people lace up for the first time in their 30s, 40s and beyond.

You don't need to have played as a kid. You do need to be able to skate. Here's the path.

How most adults get started

  1. Learn to skate. An adult group class, then lots of public skating. Our learn-to-skate guide walks you through it.
  2. Adult learn-to-play. Many rinks run beginner hockey clinics for adults. You learn stickhandling, passing and positions alongside people at your level.
  3. Stick-and-puck. Open ice to practice with a stick and puck. No game, no pressure, just you and the puck, like the old days on the driveway.
  4. Drop-in hockey. Show up, pay a fee and play pickup. Ask which sessions are beginner-friendly.
  5. Join a league. Adult leagues group teams by skill, and most have a beginner division. Some take individual sign-ups and put you on a team.

What you'll need

Drop-in and leagues require full gear: a helmet with a cage or shield, gloves, shin guards, pants, shoulder and elbow pads, a cup, skates and a stick. Used gear is perfect for your first season. Our gear checklist works for grown-ups too.

New-player etiquette

  • Most adult rec leagues are no-check. Play hard, keep it clean, everybody has work in the morning.
  • Tell the organizer you're new. Beginner divisions want you there.
  • Keep shifts short, about 60 to 90 seconds, and change on the fly.
  • Bring a light and a dark jersey to drop-in.
  • Stick around after the game. The parking lot conversations are half the fun.

Where to play

Most indoor rinks in our Utah rink directory run adult programs. Acord Ice Center in West Valley City had adult leagues and drop-in before it closed for repairs in August 2026, so expect fuller sessions at the other Salt Lake County rinks until it reopens.
