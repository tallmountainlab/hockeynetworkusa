Ask anyone who played hockey as a kid about their best memory, and a lot of them won't pick a big game. They'll talk about a frozen pond, or a backyard rink their dad flooded with a garden hose. Cold fingers, a wooden stick, the scrape of skates, someone yelling that dinner was ready. Outdoor hockey is where the game started, and where so many of us fell for it.

That is why this one matters. On Thursday, Dec. 31, 2026, the Utah Mammoth host the Colorado Avalanche in the NHL Winter Classic at Rice-Eccles Stadium. Puck drop is 4 p.m. It is the franchise's first outdoor regular-season game, against the closest thing we have to a rival, on New Year's Eve. It doesn't get much better.

Dress like you're going to the pond

The game starts in daylight, but the sun sets around 5 p.m. in late December, and the cold comes on fast after that. Plan on below freezing by the third period.

  • Layer up the way your mom made you: a thermal base layer, a fleece or sweater, then a windproof coat.
  • Warm boots and wool socks matter most. You'll be standing on cold concrete for hours.
  • Hat, gloves, and a blanket if the stadium allows it. Check the bag policy before you pack.
  • Hand warmers in every pocket. You'll thank yourself.
  • Bring a thermos if the rules allow it. If not, budget for a lot of hot chocolate.

Getting there

Rice-Eccles sits on the University of Utah campus, east of downtown. The TRAX Red Line's Stadium station is right next door, and transit will be your best friend with tens of thousands of people leaving at once. Parking on and around campus will be tight and pricey. If you drive, park downtown and ride TRAX the rest of the way.

Tickets

This will be the most sought-after hockey ticket in Utah history. Start with the official NHL and team ticket pages. If you go the resale route, stick to a site with a buyer guarantee and compare the all-in price after fees.

Watching from home

The Winter Classic airs nationally, so a living room full of family works just fine. Downtown bars and restaurants will be packed on New Year's Eve, so book a table early if you want the crowd. Or flood the backyard, lace up the old skates at the second intermission, and make a memory of your own.
