Utah Mammoth 2026-27 season guide

Year three of NHL hockey in Salt Lake City. The 84-game schedule, the dates worth circling, tickets, and how to make a night of it at Delta Center.

Updated November 1, 2026 · How we make money

If you grew up here, you probably never expected to type the words "Utah's NHL team." For decades, big-league hockey was something you watched on a fuzzy broadcast from somewhere else, or caught on a road trip. Now it is year three, the Mammoth are ours, and there is a Winter Classic on the calendar in our own backyard. It still feels a little unreal, in the best way.

Here is what you need to know for 2026-27.

The basics

  • The team: Utah Mammoth, in the Central Division. They started in 2024-25 as Utah Hockey Club and became the Mammoth for 2025-26.
  • Home ice: Delta Center, the same building where the Salt Lake Golden Eagles played their last seasons in the early '90s and the Jazz made those Finals runs.
  • The season: 84 games this year, the NHL's longest regular season in more than 30 years. It opened Oct. 1 and ends Apr. 10 in St. Louis.
  • Home openers: Utah is 2-0 all time. The first one, a 5-2 win over Chicago in October 2024, is a night a lot of us will be telling our grandkids about.

After the opener, the Mammoth play 13 of their next 18 on the road, so that mid-November homestand is the first real stretch of home cooking. Our Mammoth page has the full schedule and refreshes every morning.

Getting a ticket

Season packages started at $677 when the schedule came out. For single games, weeknights against teams from outside the division are usually the friendliest on your wallet. If you're taking a kid to their first NHL game, those are the nights to do it. You'll get to walk down close to the glass for warmups without fighting a crowd, and they'll remember it forever.

Making a night of it

  • Take TRAX. Light rail stops right by the arena, and there is something fun about a train car full of jerseys heading downtown. Use UTA's trip planner to find your line. FrontRunner riders connect at Salt Lake Central.
  • Get there for warmups. They start about 30 minutes before puck drop. Stand by the glass, listen to the pucks ring off the boards, and watch the little ones press their faces against it. That part never gets old.
  • Parking: downtown lots fill early and charge event rates. Park a few blocks out and enjoy the walk.
